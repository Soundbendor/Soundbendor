Curiosities

Questions about music and hearing we couldn't leave alone.

Beethoven's Ears · Sound Perception

How did Beethoven hear his own music?

Listen to his music the way his failing ears may have delivered it to him, from the first signs of deafness in 1801 to the year of the Ninth Symphony's premiere in 1824. Then try the same hearing loss on any sound you like.

Listen Portrait: Joseph Karl Stieler, 1820
Seeing Music · Sound Perception

What color is a C major chord?

Some composers saw colors when they heard music, and their lists don't agree. Pick a recording and watch it in Scriabin's colors, then in Rimsky-Korsakov's, then in Amy Beach's.

See it Colors: Scriabin's keys around the circle of fifths (Sabaneyev 1911)
Sine by Sine · Sound Perception

What is a sound made of?

Every sound is a stack of sine waves. Add pure tones one at a time and hear a buzz, a bell or an organ take shape. Organ builders were doing this with pipes centuries before synthesizers.

Build a sound Picture: harmonics 1 to 6 of a sawtooth and their sum
The Endless Staircase · Sound Perception

Can a sound keep rising forever?

Pitch feels simple: a sound is high or low. These sounds were built to break that rule. Listen first and decide what you hear, then see how the trick works and try to break it yourself.

Listen Picture: a Shepard scale, octave-spaced tones climbing under a fixed loudness curve
