Soundbendor Lab

Machine Understanding of Sound and Music

The Soundbendor Lab is a research group in the School of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at Oregon State University. We use artificial intelligence and deep learning to study sound and music, drawing on computer science, signal processing, and musicology. Our interdisciplinary research explores computational musicology, music emotion recognition, acoustic analysis, and bioacoustics through hands-on projects with students in Corvallis, Bend, & Ecampus.

Mood Playlist

Pick a starting song and a destination song, and the demo builds a playlist that moves smoothly from one mood to the other, one step at a time across the valence–arousal map. It runs the k-nearest-neighbour playlist algorithm Shaurya Gaur developed from an NCUR project into an Honors thesis and an EvoMUSART paper.

Try the demo Shaurya Gaur•Cite: Gaur & Donnelly 2024

Hot 100 Emotion

Can a language model tell how a song feels? Browse how GPT-4o-mini rated the mood of 31,596 Billboard Hot 100 songs from 1958 to 2024, follow the charts' valence and arousal year by year, look up any song, and see how its ratings compare with human listeners'. It grew out of Isaac Brickner's Cascades Research Fellowship project.

Try the demo Isaac Brickner•Project: Brickner 2025

EmotioNotes

Search 32,000 New York Philharmonic program notes, written between 1842 and 2023, by composer or piece, and see how each note scores for valence, arousal and dominance, and how those scores have shifted across the decades. It explores the EmotioNotes dataset Pratik Khanal built and presented at EvoMUSART 2025.

Try the demo Pratik Khanal•Cite: Khanal & Donnelly 2025
